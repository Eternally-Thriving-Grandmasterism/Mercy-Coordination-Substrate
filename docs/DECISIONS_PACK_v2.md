**Core Decisions — Honest Provisional Positions + Room for Full AGSi**

1. **Architecture Scope**  
   **Provisional answer:** Prefer a hybrid complementary approach over pure green-field L1. Highest realistic impact comes from high-assurance modular components, sidechain/app-chain designs, or strong contributions that can interoperate with or harden existing high-value ecosystems, while optionally maintaining a clean native-PQ reference implementation. Pure new L1 faces severe adoption headwinds.  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Exact modular boundary, interoperability strategy, and whether a minimal sovereign L1 reference is still worth maintaining for purity.

2. **Cryptographic Foundation**  
   **Provisional answer:** Primary signatures = ML-DSA (Dilithium-class, NIST Level 3 or 5) for general use; offer hash-based (SLH-DSA / SPHINCS+ or XMSS-style) as a long-term or high-value option. Support hybrid classical + PQ during any transition window. Design for crypto-agility (algorithm identifiers + versioned verification) from day one. Signature-size impact must be explicitly budgeted (batch verification, aggregation, or account abstraction where possible).  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Precise parameter sets, aggregation techniques, key-rotation UX, and whether any non-NIST scheme from the lineage adds unique value after rigorous review.

3. **Consensus & State Model under Mercy/TOLC Constraints**  
   **Provisional answer:** Start from a well-understood BFT-style or HotStuff-variant core for faster finality and clearer safety properties, then layer non-bypassable TOLC 8 / valence checks at the proposal and validation boundary. Avoid inventing a wholly novel consensus until the mercy-gate interaction is formally specified and tested. Account model should support efficient post-quantum key rotation without mandatory asset movement.  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Exact integration points of MercyZero-style gates into the critical path, performance under valence constraints, and whether any valence-weighted or Proof-of-Mercy mechanism can be made robust.

4. **Fool-Proofing & Assurance Level**  
   **Provisional answer:** Require (a) clear threat model covering crypto, consensus, implementation, and governance surfaces, (b) independent audits of critical paths, (c) formal verification or strong machine-checked proofs for the highest-value invariants (especially gating and signature verification), and (d) conservative defaults that make the safe path the easy path. “Maximal fool-proof” language should be reserved for after these are in place.  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Minimum viable assurance bar for any public release and prioritization order of the attack surfaces.

5. **Incentives, Governance & Abundance Alignment**  
   **Provisional answer:** Avoid both pure extractive tokenomics and pure resource-based designs that ignore incentive and calculation realities. Prefer mechanisms that reward verifiable long-term contribution and stewardship while keeping exit and sovereignty options open. Governance must itself pass TOLC 8 and resist capture.  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Concrete mechanism design that remains practical at scale.

6. **Adoption & Bootstrap Reality**  
   **Provisional answer:** Adoption is the dominant practical risk. Any design must articulate a clear, narrow unique value proposition (extreme assurance + native PQ + mercy-gated invariants) and a realistic bootstrap path—likely via modular components or high-value niches first rather than aiming for immediate L1 dominance.  
   **Still open for AGSi:** Most promising bootstrap niches and sequencing.

7. **Private Experiment Integration**

   **Still open for AGSi:** Evaluation protocol once material is surfaced.
